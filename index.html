Chinese Autonomous-Driving Research Rapidly Expands Its Presence in VLA and VLM

by Rei Kuge

Chinese companies and research institutions are becoming increasingly prominent in autonomous-driving research. An analysis of the competitiveness of Chinese research institutions, based on data from arXiv, a preprint platform that hosts artificial intelligence-related papers, has revealed that China is gaining a particularly strong presence in next-generation foundational technologies known as VLA and VLM.

To identify recent citation trends, we collected reference information from 50 papers submitted to arXiv since April 2026.
Of the 50 papers, 25 included authors affiliated with Chinese companies, universities or other institutions, while the remaining 25 did not. We then examined the distribution of references cited by each paper. In total, the papers contained approximately 2,000 references.
Our analysis of the references cited by the 50 papers found that, in 23 of them, the number of cited papers that included authors from Chinese institutions was equal to or greater than the number that included authors from U.S. institutions. One particularly interesting finding was that, among the 25 papers published without authors from Chinese institutions, six cited more papers published by Chinese institutions than papers published by U.S. institutions.

In the autonomous-driving field, expectations have risen rapidly since around 2024 for a technology known as end-to-end, or E2E, autonomous driving, in which a single AI system handles everything from perception and decision-making to vehicle control. Compared with conventional rule-based autonomous-driving technologies, E2E systems make it easier to develop responses to extreme or unusual situations. U.S. companies Tesla and Waymo were among the pioneers in this field. An analysis of the number of papers submitted to arXiv shows that the volume of research related to E2E autonomous driving has continued to rise steadily.

As efforts continue to commercialize E2E autonomous driving, attention is focusing on foundational technologies known as Vision-Language Models, or VLMs, and Vision-Language-Action models, or VLAs. A VLM is an AI model capable of processing images and language together, while a VLA builds on a VLM to also generate actions.

Research into VLMs and VLAs began to accelerate around 2024 and has since expanded rapidly. Approximately 300 papers related to E2E autonomous driving were submitted to arXiv in 2025. Of these, around 200 were related to VLMs and approximately 50 were related to VLAs. Moreover, as of July 2026, the number of VLA papers submitted in 2026 had already exceeded the total for 2025, suggesting that VLA is rapidly becoming an even more prominent buzzword.

China sees autonomous driving and other physical-AI applications as a key area of future competition and is investing heavily in their development. In its 15th Five-Year Plan, adopted in 2025, the country identified autonomous driving as a priority development area alongside large passenger aircraft and next-generation solar cells.

Among the 15 most highly cited papers in each field, 10 of the VLM papers and 11 of the VLA papers included authors affiliated with Chinese institutions.

Alongside research institutions such as Shanghai University, Tsinghua University and the Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, privately owned automakers and suppliers are also establishing a strong presence. Particularly prominent are emerging new-energy vehicle manufacturers Li Auto and Xiaomi Auto. In the VLM field, Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab, Huawei’s research organization, and major semiconductor company Horizon Robotics are also advancing research.

A paper jointly published by Li Auto and Tsinghua University in 2024 had the second-highest citation count among VLM-related studies. In the paper, the researchers proposed a technology called DriveVLM-Dual. DriveVLM-Dual improves computational speed by operating a VLM and a conventional autonomous-driving system in parallel.

Li Auto claims that the i8 electric vehicle it launched in 2025 is equipped with a VLA system. As sales growth of its range-extender electric vehicles, or REEVs, slows, the company intends to differentiate itself by accelerating the development of autonomous-driving capabilities.
